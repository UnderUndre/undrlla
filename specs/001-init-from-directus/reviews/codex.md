## Summary

The artifact set is genuinely strong for a v1 marketplace spec — 36 FRs, 19 acceptance scenarios, a real data model, a research note with 8 documented decisions, and a working OpenAPI contract. The Tier-B scope leakage that broke `claude.md`'s review is cleaned up; `copilot.md`'s partial-checkout and PII findings are addressed in spec v2. **The remaining cracks are different**: the crypto-checkout flow is not completable from the contract alone (no customer payment address returned), the collect-and-forward settlement model creates custodial/regulatory exposure that the spec never flags, guest order retrieval leaks via URL-exposed `checkout_group_id`, and the provisioning task is scoped at ~25% of its real effort. The two prior external reviews are both `HIGH` — the Principle VI gate is already blocked regardless of this review.

## Findings

| F1 | CRITICAL | Contract / payments | `contracts/openapi.yaml:86-104` `/store/checkout` returns `CheckoutGroup` → `Order` → `PaymentAttempt`, but the `PaymentAttempt` schema (`contracts/openapi.yaml:418-429`) exposes only `payout_wallet_snapshot` (the *vendor* destination). A storefront consuming this contract literally cannot complete a crypto checkout. | Add a `payment_instructions` object to the checkout response: `{ address, amount, crypto_currency, quote_expires_at, memo? }` for crypto providers. | F2 | CRITICAL | Compliance / design | `research.md:57-65` (D7) chooses **collect-and-forward** for TON/SHKeeper: customer funds land in a platform-controlled settlement wallet, platform retains the fee, vendor is paid later. This is crypto **custody** — it triggers MSB registration (US FinCEN), MiCA/CASP licensing (EU), AML/KYC obligations, and travel-rule compliance in most jurisdictions. The spec/plan/runbook mention *none* of this; the only "compliance" note (`spec.md:200`) excludes Merchant-of-Record engines on technical grounds, not legal. A client deploying this template today is silently operating an unlicensed money transmitter. | Either (a) add a compliance section flagging custodial exposure + jurisdictional gating + required licenses per instance, or (b) switch v1 to direct-to-vendor crypto payments (rejected in D7 for reconciliation simplicity — re-examine the trade-off against legal exposure), or (c) explicitly limit crypto to jurisdictions where the operator holds a license and gate at provisioning (FR-022). |

The state machine has no entry for `actor=system`. | Either (a) extend `RefundRequest` with `initiator` ∈ {customer, system} and a `system` approval bypass, or (b) model auto-refunds as a separate `AutoRefund` audit collection distinct from `RefundRequest`. ## Alternative approaches considered

- **Crypto settlement (lens G)**: D7 chose collect-and-forward over direct-to-vendor for reconciliation simplicity. Re-examining against F2: direct-to-vendor eliminates the platform's custody/AML surface at the cost of more complex fee capture and refund handling. Given that the custody model likely *blocks* commercial deployment in most jurisdictions until licensed, the trade-off should be re-weighed — the operational simplicity may not be worth the legal exposure. Flagged for the author, not prescribed.
- **Contract strategy**: `research.md` D6 splits `/store/*` (curated) from generated Directus REST/GraphQL. Reasonable, but F1/F3/F12 show the curated surface is incomplete. Alternative: generate the storefront contract from Directus OpenAPI plus a thin curated overlay — reduces drift but loses control. Current split is fine *if* the curated side is actually complete; today it is not.
- **Guest order retrieval**: F4 could be solved by issuing a non-enumerable `checkout_secret` at checkout time (separate from `checkout_group_id`) rather than adding `anon_token` to every order row. Less schema churn, same security property. Author's call.
